# Research Basis — Adherence Analytics for SmartMed

> **New to this project?** Read [ANALYSIS_EXPLAINED.md](ANALYSIS_EXPLAINED.md) first — it
> covers the same material in plain English with worked examples. This file is the formal
> citation record.

This document records the peer-reviewed literature that the analytics in this project are
derived from. SmartMed produces a **timestamped dose-event log paired with a prescribed
regimen** — the same input class used by the electronic adherence monitoring
(EAMD / MEMS) literature. Every metric computed in `dashboard/analytics.js` traces back
to a paper listed here.

**System input** (Firebase Realtime Database):

| Path | Contents |
|---|---|
| `schedules/{patientId}/{key}` | `medName`, `time` (`"HH:MM"`), `compartment`, `date`, `frequency` |
| `history/{patientId}/{key}` | `date`, `time`, `status` (`Taken`/`Missed`), `medName`, `compartment`, `scheduledTime`, `delaySec`, `epoch` |
| `patients/{patientId}/current_status` | `state`, `last_updated` |
| `patients/{patientId}/heartbeat` | `lastSeen` (unix epoch), `status` |

Events are generated by three IR beam sensors (one per compartment) on an ESP32; the
device fires an alarm at each scheduled dose time and records whether the patient
reached into the correct compartment within the monitoring window.

## Anchor papers

### P1 — ABC Taxonomy *(framing; surveyed, not implemented)*

> Vrijens B, De Geest S, Hughes DA, Przemyslaw K, Demonceau J, Ruppar T, Dobbels F,
> Fargher E, Morrison V, Lewek P, Matyjaszczyk M, Mshelia C, Clyne W, Aronson JK,
> Urquhart J; ABC Project Team. **A new taxonomy for describing and defining adherence
> to medications.** *British Journal of Clinical Pharmacology.* 2012;73(5):691–705.
> doi:[10.1111/j.1365-2125.2012.04167.x](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1365-2125.2012.04167.x)

Establishes the standard vocabulary. Adherence is a *process* with three phases:

- **Initiation** — the patient takes the first dose of a prescribed medication.
- **Implementation** — the extent to which actual dosing matches the prescribed regimen
  between initiation and the last dose (missed doses, late doses, extra doses, drug holidays).
- **Persistence** — the elapsed time between initiation and the last dose.

**Use in this project:** provides the label for what we measure. Everything implemented
here is an **Implementation**-phase metric. Initiation and Persistence metrics
(time-to-first-dose, days-since-last-dose, discontinuation) are deliberately out of scope
for this iteration and noted as the natural extension.

### P2 — EAMD Research Methods Framework *(**implemented** — the core computations)*

> McGrady ME, Ramsey RR. **Using Electronic Monitoring Devices to Assess Medication
> Adherence: a Research Methods Framework.** *Journal of General Internal Medicine.*
> 2020;35(9):2707–2714.
> doi:[10.1007/s11606-020-05905-z](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11606-020-05905-z)

The methodological backbone of this work. Defines implementation adherence as
`N / P × 100`, where `N` is medication-taking behaviour and `P` is the prescribed regimen
over a time period `t`, and specifies **three** summary statistics:

1. **% of prescribed doses taken** — `doses taken / doses prescribed`
2. **% of prescribed doses taken on time** — `doses taken within a predetermined timing
   window / doses prescribed` (the paper gives ±60 minutes as an example window)
3. **% of days with correct doses** — a dichotomous per-day indicator, `days with the
   correct dose count / total days`

**Critical procedural requirement:** the statistic is computed **at the daily level first,
then averaged** across the aggregation period — *not* pooled over the whole window. A
patient who takes 6 doses on Monday and 0 on Tuesday does not score 100% for the pair.

The paper also specifies the data-cleaning rules this project implements:

- **Repeat-actuation removal** — multiple actuations within a 30-minute window reflect
  user error or device malfunction, not multiple doses. Retain only the first.
  (The paper's worked example: triggers at 8:03:10, 8:04:12 and 8:04:33 count as **one**
  dose at 8:03:10.) Directly applicable here — an IR beam is easily broken twice while
  a patient retrieves one pill.
- **Non-use exclusion** — days of legitimate non-use (hospitalisation, prescription held)
  must be removed from the analysis so they do not count against the patient. This is
  distinct from non-adherence.
- **Upper limit** — daily values may optionally be capped at 100% to prevent
  over-consumption inflating the average.

### P3 — AdhereR *(surveyed; next extension)*

> Dima AL, Dediu D. **Computation of adherence to medication and visualization of
> medication histories in R with AdhereR: Towards transparent and reproducible use of
> electronic healthcare data.** *PLOS ONE.* 2017;12(4):e0174426.
> doi:[10.1371/journal.pone.0174426](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0174426)

Implements nine Continuous Medication Availability (CMA) metrics, plus two techniques
relevant to a longitudinal dashboard:

- **Sliding-window CMA** — adherence computed across fixed consecutive or overlapping
  windows, rather than one figure for an entire observation period. Addresses the fact
  that a single number hides a patient whose adherence was perfect for two months and
  erratic for the third.
- **Treatment-episode detection** — consecutive events belong to the same episode of
  active medication use unless the gap between them exceeds a user-defined threshold.
  This is the mechanism behind drug-holiday detection.

**Status:** not implemented. The daily table produced in `analytics.js` is the correct
substrate for both — a rolling window and a gap detector read directly off it.

### P4 — Variation by calculation method *(surveyed; motivates our reporting choice)*

> Rohay JM, Dunbar-Jacob JM. **Variation in adherence measures as a function of
> calculation methods.** *Frontiers in Pharmacology.* 2024;15:1460327.
> doi:[10.3389/fphar.2024.1460327](https://doi.org/10.3389/fphar.2024.1460327)

Applied three calculation methods to the **same** electronically monitored patients over
six months:

| Method | Adherence rate | Within-patient monthly SD |
|---|---|---|
| PILLCOUNT (count ÷ expected, ignoring timing) | 89–92% | — |
| DAILY (per-day count ÷ per-day prescribed) | 79–85% | 20–25% |
| **TIMING** (accounts for inter-dose intervals) | **62–68%** | **29–35%** |

Two findings drive design decisions here:

1. The choice of method moves the headline number by ~25 percentage points on identical
   behaviour. **A single adherence percentage is not interpretable without its
   definition.** This is why the dashboard displays taking adherence and timing adherence
   *side by side*, and why the Methods panel states the numerator, denominator and
   timing window for each.
2. Timing adherence has higher within-patient variability than daily adherence —
   patients vary *when* they dose more than *whether* they dose. Timing is therefore the
   more sensitive signal for a caregiver.

**Status:** the side-by-side reporting is implemented. The variability statistic
(monthly SD) is not.

## Supporting reference

Not an anchor paper; cited for the pattern analyses that the hour-of-day heatmap visualises.

> Burnier M. **Is There a Threshold for Medication Adherence? Lessons Learnt From
> Electronic Monitoring of Drug Adherence.** *Frontiers in Pharmacology.* 2018;9:1540.
> doi:[10.3389/fphar.2018.01540](https://doi.org/10.3389/fphar.2018.01540)

Describes the characteristic patterns that electronic monitoring — and only electronic
monitoring — can reveal: isolated and sequential omissions, **drug holidays**
(discontinuation for 24–72 h), the **weekend effect**, white-coat adherence (improved
dosing in the days before an appointment), and dumping. These are the patterns the
hour-of-day × weekday miss heatmap is designed to surface.

## Metric implementation map

| Metric | Formula | Anchor | Implemented |
|---|---|---|---|
| Taking adherence | mean over days of `N / P × 100` | P2 | Yes |
| Timing adherence | mean over days of `N_onTime / P × 100`, window ±60 min | P2, P4 | Yes |
| Correct-dosing days | `days where N = P / total days × 100` | P2 | Yes |
| Repeat-actuation dedup | first event per 30-min window | P2 | Yes |
| Non-use exclusion | drop flagged days from numerator *and* denominator | P2 | Yes |
| Miss rate by hour × weekday | misses / prescribed, per 7×24 cell | Burnier 2018 | Yes |
| Sliding-window CMA | rolling window over the daily table | P3 | No |
| Drug-holiday / gap detection | runs of consecutive days with no dose | P3, Burnier 2018 | No |
| Within-patient variability | monthly SD of daily adherence | P4 | No |
| Initiation / persistence | time-to-first-dose, days-since-last-dose | P1 | No |

Metrics marked "No" are documented deliberately: they are the surveyed literature this
implementation does *not* yet cover, and each has a defined path to implementation off
the existing daily table.
